At Allo Health we replaced a rate-limited Zoho Inventory dependency with an in-house pharmacy inventory and billing system. The core shipped in about eight weeks. The decision that shaped everything else was small: **the place where stock sits is not the same thing as who owns it.**

## The problem with "stock at a location"

Most off-the-shelf inventory tools treat a warehouse or store as the unit of ownership. If a box is on the shelf, it belongs to that shelf.

Pharmacy distribution doesn't work like that. One batch of medicine can move from a wholesaler to a franchise to a patient. Along the way it can sit somewhere it isn't owned, and the same batch can be valued differently depending on who owns it. The old tool simply couldn't represent that.

## Two dimensions, not one

We modelled ownership and location as separate dimensions on stock. A batch has a place where it physically is and an entity that owns it, and its valuation follows the owner. Once that was true, multi-entity valuation stopped being a special case.

## Money lives on the server

We also moved pricing and tax server-side: discount rules, date-aware GST and the MRP cap. Clients display numbers but never compute them.

## Tests at the seams

Unit tests passed while real flows were still broken. End-to-end tests covering both B2B and B2C flows caught **five defects that the unit tests missed**. For billing, the end-to-end test is the one that matters.
